08 — Voice & Messaging

Brand voice

British English in narrative prose. International/neutral English in field names, code, JSON, and metadata. Professional tone — no superlatives, no hype.

Mission statement
"Making the web — and everything you publish beyond it — work for everyone and everything that uses it."

Core phrases (use verbatim)

PhraseContext
Machine ExperienceDiscipline name — always capitalised, never abbreviated to "MX" in first use
Structure for machines. Explicit for everyone.Hero/acrostic copy — the MX = Machine Experience visual pun
Make anything you publish readable by machines.Value proposition — scope is all formats, not just web pages
MX: The Intro / MX: The Handbook / MX: The ProtocolsBook series — always in this exact form with colon
Consultancy, training, books, and toolsFour-pillar offering — use in that order
CogNovaMX — Machine ExperienceLogo alt text — verbatim
Do
British English in narrative prose: organise, colour, recognised, programme
First-person voice: "MX adds…", "The Gathering defines…"
Short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence.
Timeless present tense in manuscripts — no "new in this version"
Answer-first structure: conclusion before explanation
Neutral English in field names, JSON, code: license, color, organization
Don't
Superlatives or hype: "revolutionary", "game-changing", "world-class"
Hard-coded counts: "all 10 submodules" → "all submodules"
Publication dates in manuscript body: "this update includes…"
Passive voice when active is available
Naming MX as a tool or plugin — it is a standard
Claiming MX duplicates Schema.org, Dublin Core, or WCAG
