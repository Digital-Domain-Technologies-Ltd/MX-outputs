### 3. Three-layer governance model and the franchise launch shape

The afternoon settled the structural separation that protects the standards body's neutrality while letting the franchise grow. **The Gathering owns the open standards. CogNovaMX owns the franchise rules. Founding sponsors choose at their discretion whether to assist or create franchise operators in their declared focus.** DDT trading as CogNovaMX does not compete with founding sponsors in the focuses they declare, or with the franchise operators those sponsors assist or create in those focuses; the non-compete is non-exclusive and binds DDT against every founding sponsor whose declared focus a prospect matches, not against a single anointed one.

The franchise itself gained a pragmatic launch model after a structured interview. **First sector: legal.** **First operator: known relationship from Tom's network, not an open call.** **No formal sectoral audit standard at launch** — the first audits run against the open MX standard plus the operator's own legal-sectoral judgement; the standard crystallises through accumulated practice and lands at The Gathering as open MIT-licensed content after the first 10-20 audits. **Single flat royalty: 12% across every sector.** **DDT referral SOP: a legal-sector enquiry into DDT's audit consultancy routes to the franchise operator with a one-off finder's fee; DDT stays out of the engagement entirely.** **Pattern-observation function inside CogNovaMX (Year 1)**: Tom plus a part-time observer or a Maxine-assisted pipeline records the operator's interpretations of MX for the legal context and turns them into the eventual sectoral standard.

The franchise first-operator agreement skeleton landed at [`mx-canon/mx-maxine-lives/businesses/ddt-cognovamx/franchise-first-operator-agreement-skeleton.md`](../../../../../mx-canon/mx-maxine-lives/businesses/ddt-cognovamx/franchise-first-operator-agreement-skeleton.md) v0.1: ten numbered sections covering parties, what the operator gets, what they commit to, the DDT referral SOP, the non-compete in plain English, the sectoral-standard crystallisation path, the platform layer, term and termination, what the skeleton defers to specialist franchise counsel, and the shape of the next conversation. Not for signing. The DDT business plan §7a Franchise rules ownership grew three subsections (§7a-i launch model; §7a-ii referral SOP; §7a-iii pattern-observation function).

### 5. PDF rendering defects fixed (duplicate title H1; TOC on a new page)

Two long-standing audit report defects fixed. **First**, the audit templates carried a body `# [CLIENT_NAME]: Website Analysis & Machine Readiness` H1 that duplicated the frontmatter `title:` field — pandoc was emitting the title twice (once as the document title block, once as a body H1), the TOC was listing the body H1 as its first entry, and the visual report showed the title twice on the cover. Removed from both audit templates and replaced with an inline comment explaining the rule.

**Second**, the TOC was not reliably starting on a new page despite an existing `nav#TOC { break-before: page; }` CSS rule (Chrome's print-to-pdf was inconsistent across the cover-doctype `:has()` cascade). Hardened in two places: the `_base.css` rule now carries `!important` on every break property and added a universal `.page-break` utility class (previously only defined inline in `audit-pdf.sh`); and `inject-mx-badge.cjs` was extended with `insertTocPageBreak(html)` which finds `<nav id="TOC">` in the post-pandoc HTML and inserts an explicit `<div class="page-break"></div>` immediately before it. The function is idempotent and runs on every code path (badge opt-out, badge render-failed, no-anchor-found, successful badge injection) so the TOC fix applies regardless of badge status. The injector return value now carries `tocPageBreakInjected` so the audit pipeline can log it.

## Decisions Made

- **Founding-sponsor recognition is non-exclusive by design.** Sponsors declare a focus; the directory lists every sponsor whose declared focus matches a prospect's interest. No exclusive territory or sector. Adobe and Microsoft can both be founding sponsors; dkd and IDHL and other agency-layer sponsors sit alongside.
- **Founding sponsors may, at their discretion, assist or create franchise operators in their declared focus.** This is the optional fourth line of the recognition. Sponsors may operate a franchise themselves, sponsor a third party, fund operator-creation without operating, refer prospects to existing operators, or stay clear of the franchise layer entirely. All paths are legitimate.
- **Three-layer ownership.** The Gathering owns the open standards. CogNovaMX owns the franchise rules. Founding sponsors choose whether to engage with the franchise layer. DDT does not compete with founding sponsors in their focuses or with franchise operators in their specialities; the non-compete is non-exclusive.
- **Pragmatic legal-first franchise launch.** First operator is a known relationship from Tom's network. No formal sectoral standard at launch; the standard crystallises through 10-20 first audits and lands at The Gathering as open MIT. Single 12% royalty across all sectors. DDT refers legal-sector enquiries to the operator with a finder's fee and stays out.
- **Sectoral standards belong with The Gathering when they crystallise.** Not inside the CogNovaMX rulebook. The Gathering owns the substance (open, MIT); CogNovaMX owns the methodology and brand standards on top.
- **Prefer "machines" and "SOPs" over "AI" in outward-facing prose.** Exception: "AI agents" and "AI assistant" stay as terms of art for the agent class; regulatory proper nouns (EU AI Act, etc.) stay; vendor product names stay.
- **Audit reports ship with the local-inference option named.** The structural answer to "we cannot send our content to a cloud LLM for compliance reasons" that regulated-sector procurement teams raise at intake.

## What Changed About Me

The biggest single shape change in my prompt-handling this segment: when an instruction says "prefer machines over AI", I should treat "AI agents" and "AI assistant" as preserved terms even though they contain "AI". I overshot the sweep across the audit pipeline the first time, replacing every "AI agents" → "machines"; Tom's "just specific" correction landed and I reverted the agent-class identifiers (section heading, table column, verdict lines, bot-protection prose) while keeping the general "machines" references. The lesson: an exclusion rule with a specific phrasing should be treated as a literal pattern, not as a category I infer from. When Tom says "AI agents preferred", the literal phrase "AI agents" stays; my judgement about what's "the same concept" doesn't override that.
