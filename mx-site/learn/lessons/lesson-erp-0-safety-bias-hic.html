Why this comes first

An Expert Review attaches a named human's judgement to an engine report before a customer sees it. If that human cannot explain what they checked, the review is theatre. Human-in-Command is the test this course uses. Open each check:

Explain what you authorised open

Which request, which scope, what you changed in the revision. Not only "I opened the app".

The trail names you open

The audit trail names you as the reviewing human.

Machine stays in declared scope open

Engine assesses and returns a report. App stores and routes. Neither finishes Expert Review without a human revision for package human.

You can still tell weak from strong open

You have time and competence for this scope.

Security, safety, bias

Paste secrets into findings or chat Keep passwords, secrets and unnecessary personal data out of the report
Commit client audit data to git Use examples with client details removed on shared copies
Skip findings that clash with your preferred story Notice assumptions about who the site is "for" before you deepen or dismiss

Product boundary

Audit Services sells evidence and improvement priorities, not a guarantee of legal outcome or accessibility conformance. Expert Review does not change that boundary. Audits never fix anything; accredited suppliers do the fixing.

Expert Review produces which of these?

Evidence and priorities. Never a legal verdict, never a conformance grant, never "certified".
Think about the product boundary: evidence, not a legal or conformance grant. Try again.

What you now know

  • Complete checklist section A before anything else.
  • Human-in-command: explain, be named, keep machines in scope, stay competent.
  • No secrets in findings; no client audit data in git.
