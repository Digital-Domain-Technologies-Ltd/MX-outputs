REGINALD

The registry that makes MX-attested content verifiable.

MX makes content machine-readable. REGINALD makes it machine-trustworthy. Both properties are required for machine-ready content; building one without the other is building half the system.

Content Ops is the discipline of creating, managing, improving, publishing, distributing, archiving, and retiring content across every digital channel. MX is the layer that keeps Content Ops work usable when an AI agent, or any other system, encounters the file outside the environment that produced it. REGINALD is the registry that signs it.

REGINALD is the public registry where documents are registered, cryptographically signed, and made verifiable by any machine on earth. A traditional proper name that describes exactly what it does:

  • Registry for
  • Genuine
  • Information,
  • Notarised
  • Authentication, and
  • Legitimate
  • Documentation.

The attestation is narrow and precise: this is what the owner published, unaltered. Origin and integrity only, not factual correctness, not editorial quality.

What a machine can actually check

"Trustworthy" is a word, and a machine cannot act on a word. It can act on four separate questions, and REGINALD is built to answer each one on its own.

Attestation. Who published this, and when? A signature bound to a declared identity, not a claim in the page's own text.

Currency. Is this the version that still stands, or one superseded three revisions ago?

Freshness. Is the publisher still behind it? A record whose owner has walked away is a different thing from one they renew, and a machine should be able to tell them apart.

Corroboration. Does an authoritative outside source independently say the same thing? Attestation proves who published a claim. It never proves anyone credible agrees with it, and pretending otherwise is how a signed lie gets treated as a fact.

The part that survives us

Here is the difference that matters, and it is the one no governance platform can offer you.

A governance platform keeps your evidence inside its own product. You query it while you hold a licence, export it at their discretion, and lose it the day you leave. Ours travels inside the file. The signature, the provenance, and the record of how it was made go wherever the document goes, and a regulator, an auditor, or an acquirer verifies them on their own machine using standard cryptographic libraries they already have.

They need no account with us. They need no permission from us. They do not have to trust us at all, which is precisely the point: verification you have to take our word for is not verification.

REGINALD signs. It does not verify. Verification is yours, and everybody else's.

The practical effect compounds. Agents that read attested documents hallucinate less, because they have verified facts to cite rather than inferences to make. Fewer inference steps means lower token consumption and lower energy draw. As the EU AI Act, the European Accessibility Act, and digital-records legislation across multiple jurisdictions place documentation, logging, and verifiability obligations on the bodies they cover, attestation becomes the layer that makes the required documentation verifiable on request. MX and REGINALD do not grant compliance with any of these regulations; that remains a legal duty of the publishing body. What they do is make the documentation that body must produce structured, machine-readable, tamper-evident, and verifiable on request.

One of those obligations has a date on it. From 2 August 2026, EU AI Act Article 50 requires that AI-generated output is marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated, text included. The high-risk conformity obligations follow later, from 2 December 2027, after the Digital Omnibus deferral the Council approved in June 2026. The marking duty is the near one, and the evidence chain it needs is the same one the conformity assessment will ask for afterwards, which means the work is not thrown away.
