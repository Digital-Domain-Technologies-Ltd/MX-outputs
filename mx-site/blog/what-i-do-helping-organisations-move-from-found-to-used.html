What I Do: Helping Organisations Move From Found to Used

1 May 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 4 min read

Most teams today are still optimising for visibility. SEO to get found. GEO, more recently, to get cited by AI systems. Both are useful. Neither solves the deeper problem: machines still cannot use most organisational content.

That is the gap I work in. I help organisations move past tactical optimisation and into Machine Experience, the structural layer that makes content readable, trustworthy, and actionable for any machine, on any platform, in any context.

The future is not about being seen by AI systems. It is about being used by them. What follows is how I help organisations make that move.

MX audits, see what AI systems actually see

An MX audit shows how your content reads to the machines that now sit between you and your audience: AI-generated answers, retrieval pipelines, agents acting on a user's behalf, voice interfaces, private knowledge bases, training corpora.

The audit reports the structural gaps that block trust, reuse, and action, and gives you a prioritised remediation plan that you can hand to a content team or a development team and actually run. It is the fastest route I know to understanding why content underperforms in AI systems, and what to fix first.

If you want a worked argument for the return on this kind of work, why an MX audit pays for itself sets it out.

The MX books, the playbook for the machine-readable future

I have written the MX book series, a three-volume body of work that defines Machine Experience as a discipline:

  • MX: The Intro, the business case and the urgency.
  • MX: The Handbook, the practical and operational guidance.
  • MX: The Protocols, the specification and ruleset for machine-readable content.

The series gives a team a shared language, a shared model, and a shared direction. It is the document a head of content can hand to a head of engineering and have the same conversation start in the same place. The books page has the current cover set and ordering details.

MX architecture and metadata design, build content machines can use

GEO works on the surface. MX works underneath. I help organisations redesign their content systems so the assets become:

  • Portable, not trapped inside a CMS template.
  • Self-describing, so the metadata travels with the document.
  • Verifiable, through Reginald notarisation, currently in beta.
  • Consistent across every machine context, not only the one currently in fashion.

This sits on top of the cog format, the community-governed standard for documents that machines can read directly. If your content has to survive ingestion into RAG, training corpora, agent commerce flows, or long-term archives, this is the foundation that survives the next platform shift.

Accessibility and AI readiness convergence

MX collapses workstreams that used to be separate. Accessibility (WCAG, PDF/UA, the European Accessibility Act), machine-readability, and editorial governance can run as one coherent process rather than three.

The result is lower cost, fewer handoffs, and content that works for everyone reading it, human or machine. The Convergence Principle is the same one that runs through the books: interfaces designed properly for machines turn out to be better for humans too. A document an agent can act on is also a document a screen reader handles cleanly and a translator can localise without losing meaning.

The Gathering, the community behind the standard

I founded The Gathering, the open community that stewards:

  • The cog metadata standard.
  • The Reginald notarisation protocol (open format; Reginald is one implementation).
  • The MX protocols specification.
  • The shared governance model for machine-readable content.

For an organisation, this is the part that single-vendor offerings cannot match: a standards-backed, community-governed foundation for long-term AI readiness, with a public review process and a published draft set. tg.community is the door.

Strategic advisory and mentoring

For organisations building AI-ready content systems, agent-compatible services, enterprise knowledge bases, or long-term content governance, I provide ongoing advisory support: helping teams adopt MX patterns, build internal capability, and align their content with the direction the web is actually moving rather than the direction last quarter's marketing predicted.
