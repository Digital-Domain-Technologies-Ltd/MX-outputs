How an AX Audit Proves Itself

28 May 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 4 min read

Audit reports usually carry their own credibility problem. The reader has the document. They don't have the evidence that produced it. They can ask the author, who can show their working, and that working may or may not match what shipped. An AX (agent experience) audit closes that gap from the other side. An auditor reading an MX deliverable can walk from the document to every signal it depends on, without needing our word for anything.

Evidence rides inside the PDF

Every audit PDF holds a structured XMP packet in its metadata stream. The packet records the title in dc:title, and it gains the PDF/UA-1 identification (pdfuaid:part=1, ISO 14289-1) only when the finished document passes validation. One that fails goes without, rather than claiming what it hasn't earned.

On a contract, or with the evidence add-on, the same packet embeds the full provenance chain under XMP-mx:ProvenanceAiPayload: every step that touched the report, who or what carried it out, what went in, what verdict came out, and the AI-governance frameworks that apply to each (EU AI Act, UK ICO AI guidance, NIST AI RMF, Colorado AI Act). The chain also travels as a pair of files, the AI record (<basename>.provenance.ai.json) and its deterministic companion for the rule-driven steps, carried inside the PDF as associated files. An inspector who has only the PDF has everything.

To extract the embedded chain: exiftool -b -XMP-mx:ProvenanceAiPayload <file>.pdf. The -b flag matters; without it exiftool prepends a label and the JSON doesn't parse.

Every report is checked before it ships

Before release, every fact a report states is checked against the results the audit recorded. A disagreement stops the PDF, so every part of a delivered report agrees with the rest and with the results behind it. Nothing is papered over with a warning.

One scale, stated scope

The report uses one scale throughout: Good, Fair or Poor, with a grade alongside, from each row up to the overall figure. Beside each one, the report states what it covers, and its Scope and Limits section says what the audit looked at and what it didn't. A machine-readable JSON export of the results ships with every audit, so an auditor can set its figures against the report's tables without retyping anything.

A regression test holds the PDF contract

"MX Compatible" is a regression-tested contract, not a marketing line. A test renders a small fixture through the same PDF pipeline that produces client deliveries and asserts every signal an inspector relies on:

  • The AI provenance file exists beside the PDF.
  • The deterministic provenance file exists beside the PDF.
  • The XMP packet carries dc:title, and a PDF/UA-1 identification only when the file passed validation, never an unchecked one.
  • The MX identity fields in XMP mirror the source frontmatter byte-for-byte.
  • The provenance pointer fields name both files by basename.
  • The full AI evidence chain is embedded inline under XMP-mx:ProvenanceAiPayload.
  • The inline copy matches the AI provenance file after JSON normalisation.

It runs whenever the pipeline is modified, so an edit that would break the MX Compatible contract fails the test instead of reaching a client.

What an auditor can do with this

An AX audit report can be verified without leaving the document. exiftool -b -XMP-mx:ProvenanceAiPayload <file>.pdf | jq . returns the full chain on the command line, and the deterministic companion is named in the same packet under XMP-mx:ProvenanceCompanion. The frameworks each step is recorded against are named in the chain itself, so an auditor working through a regulatory clause can find the steps the report cites for it.

An auditor who prefers a browser to a terminal can use the interactive inspector at /tools/pdf-inspector.html. It accepts any MX PDF and walks the full evidence chain in the browser. A reader who wants the short version of what each signal means, and which standard it cites, can read the explainer at /learn/mx-for-pdfs.html.

Note: This page describes regulatory frameworks in general terms only. Nothing here is legal advice. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, organisation type, and use case. Consult qualified legal specialists for guidance specific to your situation.

None of this constitutes a compliance claim against any specific regulation. Conformance with the EAA or the EU AI Act, and accountability to the ICO, remain legal duties of the publishing organisation. What MX provides is the evidence vehicle: the structured, tamper-evident, machine-readable trail that the organisation needs to produce when asked. An auditor walking that trail can lean on the PDF, the inspector, the explainer, the provenance files, the scope statement and the framework citations. That's the contract.
