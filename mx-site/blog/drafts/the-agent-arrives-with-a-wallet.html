The Agent Arrives With a Wallet

12 June 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 5 min read
  • Machine Experience
  • Agentic Commerce
  • Payments
  • Visa
  • Chatgpt
  • Agents
  • Mx

On 10 June 2026, at its Payments Forum in San Francisco, Visa announced it has embedded its payment network inside ChatGPT. Ortutay and Sweet (2026) reported the arrangement for AP: link your Visa card to the chatbot, and an agent can complete a purchase on your behalf at potentially any merchant that accepts Visa. OpenAI supplies the agent. Visa approves the payment and watches for fraud.

The detail that matters is the previous attempt. OpenAI's Instant Checkout asked merchants to integrate and pay a fee for the privilege. Adoption stayed thin, transactions misfired, and AP reports OpenAI retired it in March 2026. Visa inverted the model. The merchant does nothing. The agent arrives with a payment instrument the till has accepted for decades.

That inversion is the story. Here's what the deal gives each of you, and what it asks each of you to carry.

What Everyone Gains

If you shop through an agent, the errand finally completes. "Buy the blue one in a medium" now ends in a confirmation, not a handoff to a checkout you fill in yourself. The protections come from the card rather than from a chatbot experiment: fraud monitoring you already rely on, and spending limits Visa says are coming.

If you read agent answers, the answer and the action are now the same conversation. The gap between "which kettle should I buy" and one on its way has closed. Recommendations that end in an act tend to get sharper, because a wrong one now costs somebody money.

If you sell, this is reach without an onboarding project. The first wave of agent commerce was an opt-in club with an integration bill; this one includes every till that takes the card, from day one, with nothing to build. Businesses gain a second channel too: agents approved to procure items and handle the invoicing.

What Each of You Now Carries

Shoppers carry the gap between a settled payment and a correct purchase. The agent buys what it read, and the payment clears even when the variant is wrong. One credential now stands behind every purchase your agent makes, and how a dispute works when the machine misread a product page is an open question.

Readers carry the incentives. The financial terms of this arrangement are undisclosed. When a recommendation can end in a settled transaction, you want to know whether the answer was chosen for you or for the settlement. I'm not claiming the answers are bent. I'm saying nobody outside the deal can currently check.

Sellers carry orders nobody designed the page to receive. There was no opt-in, so there's no opt-out to point at when an agent misreads your catalogue and generates a real order, a real return, and a real support ticket. The conversation that used to happen on your site now happens in the chat window, and the customer relationship thins to a delivery address.

The Funnel Was Never Just a Path to the Till

There's a quieter loss for the seller, and it deserves its own paragraph. The path to a purchase was never only friction to remove. A shopper heading for one product wanders: a side trip to the sale rail, a hover over the bundle, a pause on the reviews, three minutes of dwell that build a brand without anyone clicking buy. Each of those moments creates value. The impulse add lifts the basket. The hover tells your analytics what nearly sold. The newsletter box catches the address that funds next season's campaign.

An agent takes none of those detours. It fetches the page, settles the payment, and leaves; the order arrives and the wander never happened. No second item discovered, no signal captured, no audience built. The till rings and the shop learns nothing. That's the trade hiding inside the convenience: conversion without the funnel is revenue without the relationship, and the sellers who feel it first will be the ones whose margins lived in the basket rather than the item.

The Part Nobody Solved

Visa solved the payment. Nobody solved the reading. The agent can pay anywhere, but it can only buy what it can read, and most product pages were written for eyes. Prices locked inside images. Variants that only make sense once the photo loads. Returns policies buried in a PDF.

So here's the concrete move. Open your highest-value product page and read it as text: the structure, the headings, the metadata, the plain words. Can a reader with no eyes find the price, the right variant, the returns policy? If yes, the agent that arrives with a wallet buys correctly, and every purchase strengthens the channel. If no, fix that page before the traffic finds it. The wallet is already on its way.
