When the CDN Goes Dark

17 June 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 3 min read

Imagine this. It is a Tuesday morning. Your company has spent eight months building AI visibility - citations in ChatGPT, recommendations in Perplexity, brand mentions in every agent-powered search. Real traction. Hard-won.

Then your CDN contract lapses.

Or someone changes provider. Or the integration silently breaks during a platform migration. It does not matter which. The result is the same: your AI readiness reverts to zero. Overnight. No warning in your content. No trace in your files. The governance was never yours to keep.

This is the vendor dependency trap.

How CDN-Layer AI Readability Works

Tools like Scrunch's Agent Experience Platform (AXP) work by intercepting HTTP requests at the CDN edge. When an AI crawler hits your site, the platform detects it and serves optimised, structured content instead of the raw page. Clever engineering. Real results in the short term.

But notice what is missing. Nothing changed in your actual content. Your HTML is the same. Your PDFs are the same. Your markdown files are the same. The intelligence was applied over the content, not into it.

The governance lived in the vendor layer.

The Moment the Lights Go Out

Cancel the subscription. Switch CDN providers. Get acquired. Merge platforms. Suffer a billing outage. Any of these is enough.

The AI bots arrive the next morning and find what was always there: unstructured content with no machine-readable governance. The months of citations your team built do not transfer. They were downstream of the vendor layer, not downstream of the document.

That is not an edge case. That is the fundamental architecture.

Governance That Travels

A COG - a Community Owned Governance Standard - works differently. It is written into the document at the point of creation. Embedded in the YAML frontmatter. Encoded in PDF XMP metadata. Declared in the HTML head.

Wherever the document goes, the governance goes with it.

Cancel a subscription. The COG is still there. Change CDN. The COG is still there. Scrape the file into a RAG pipeline, email it as a PDF, archive it in the Wayback Machine. The COG is still there. It does not rely on any infrastructure layer staying live. It relies on the document existing.

REGINALD attestation follows the same principle. A signed COG is cryptographically verifiable indefinitely. The certificate does not expire when a vendor does.

The Architectural Principle

Infrastructure is temporary. Documents are permanent.

Governance that lives in infrastructure inherits infrastructure's failure modes: contract risk, migration risk, acquisition risk, outage risk. Governance that lives in the document inherits nothing except the durability of the file format.

There is a reason we do not store a book's title in the bookshelf.

What This Means for Your AI Strategy

If your AI readability depends on a layer you are paying for monthly, it is a subscription. The moment you stop paying, the machines stop understanding you.

If your AI readability is written into your content, it is an asset. It compounds. It survives everything short of deleting the file itself.

Build it into the document. Not over it.

That is what MX is for.
