Watching the Machines

29 May 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 3 min read

A running record of how the companies building and shipping AI behave toward the people whose devices, content, and attention they depend on, read through Machine Experience. When an actor crosses a line worth recording, or a regulator draws one, it goes here with the evidence, named and dated.

This is not general AI news. There is plenty of that elsewhere. The column exists for the questions Machine Experience is built around, and it is held to one test: is this something only an MX reading could say. If a piece turns on consent, provenance, attestation, accountability, accessibility, or the human staying in the loop, it belongs here. If it is a model release or a benchmark, it does not.

Failures, fixes, and the line being drawn

What broke: a specific behaviour by a named actor, with the evidence and the principle it offends. What good looks like: the right kind of answer when someone builds one, so the column rewards the behaviour it wants more of. And the line being drawn: enforcement and regulation read through MX readiness, because what a regulator decides this year shapes what every publisher has to show next year.

The standard in summary

The column names companies and developments, so it holds itself to a written standard: primary sources, dated and linked; claims attributed; the actor's own rationale represented before it is criticised; rulings and corrections acknowledged; no invented quotes; and an interest declared, since CogNovaMX sells the discipline the column argues for. Every entry ends with the constructive answer rather than the grievance. The full standard is here: How Watching the Machines Works.

Watching the Machines holds itself to the same standard. A watch that argues for evidence you can audit has to be auditable.

The record

  • The Silent Install, a series on what a program may do to your device without asking, opened by Chrome writing a multi-gigabyte AI model to people's disks with no prompt. The principle, the mechanism, and a plain look at the company at the centre of the case, with attestation and provenance as the answer.

More entries will be added here as they are published.
