Building a Company's Grant Application in One Session

8 February 2026 · Tom Cranstoun and Maxine · 6 min read
  • Blog
  • Grant
  • Reginald

How MX Reginald Ltd went from a technical proposal to a complete grant-ready package in a single Claude Code session.

Date: 8 February 2026 Author: Tom Cranstoun Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 4.6 (Maxine)

What happened

On 7 February 2026, a technical document existed: a pilot proposal for REGINALD, a verified documentation registry designed to reduce AI inference waste. It was a good document — detailed, data-driven, aimed at AI provider engineering leads. But it was one document. A company needs more than one document.

On 8 February, in a single working session with Maxine (Claude Code), we built the entire operational foundation for MX Reginald Ltd's grant application to the Scottish Government. From a standing start to 17 deliverables, a demo website, and a clear strategy for securing £250k+ in funding.

This is what MX in practice looks like.

What we started with

  • reginald.md — A technical pilot proposal aimed at AI providers (existed before this session)
  • reginald.pdf — PDF version of the same

That was it. One technical document and its PDF.

What we built

Session 1: Company identity (previous session)

  • README.md — What MX Reginald Ltd does and why it exists
  • pr/company-launch.md — Press release for company formation
  • pr/product-launch.md — Press release for REGINALD general availability (12 May 2026, CMS Summit Frankfurt)

Session 2: Grant application package (this session)

The interview. Before writing a single word, Maxine interviewed me. Nine questions, each surfaced with multiple-choice options. Company structure? Sister company, same owner. Grant targeting? Need recommendations. Headcount? Flexible with funding. Scott McGregor's role? Co-founder and director. Why Glasgow? Tech ecosystem and universities. Budget range? £250k+. Demo scope? Both interactive and static. Existing traction? All of it — live registry, pilot conversations, books in progress, community.

The interview took three minutes. It shaped everything that followed.

Strategy and tracking (4 documents):

  • grant/plan.md — Month-by-month grant strategy. Layer university seed funding (£5-10k), feasibility study (£100k), RSA job creation (£250-500k), Innovate UK R&D (£250-500k). Total target: £610k-£1.1M across stacked grants.
  • grant/budget.md — Three-year financial summary. Year 1: £345k. Year 2: £466k. Year 3: £666k. Revenue exceeds costs in Year 3. Break-even Month 18-24.
  • grant/tracker.md — Ten grants identified, tracked, and sequenced. RSA, SMART:SCOTLAND, Innovate UK Smart Grants, Scottish National Investment Bank, Interface Innovation Vouchers, and more.
  • grant/risk-register.md — Eleven risks scored by likelihood and impact. Highest: AI providers build their own (score 6), market doesn't adopt MX standards (score 6). Both mitigated by two-year head start and MIT-licensed specifications.

Evidence and validation (3 documents):

  • grant/market-research.md — $10-30B problem by 2030. TAM/SAM/SOM analysis. Competitive landscape showing REGINALD's differentiation from RAG, fine-tuning, web search, and prompt engineering.
  • grant/traction.md — Everything that exists today: live registry, operational signing engine, two pilots (digital + physical media), Dotfusion case study, advisory board of four, two-year publication track record, active AI provider pilot conversations.
  • grant/letters-of-support.md — Four templates (industry expert, academic partner, client, industry body) plus an 11-row tracker for obtaining letters before grant deadlines.

Alignment and impact (3 documents):

  • grant/scottish-alignment.md — REGINALD mapped against seven Scottish Government priorities: AI strategy, net zero, digital transformation, fair work, inclusive growth, export potential, and innovation. Each with specific contribution statements.
  • grant/impact-framework.md — Theory of change diagram. Measurable targets across commercial, employment, environmental, and innovation outcomes. Quarterly reporting commitments.
  • grant/sustainability.md — How REGINALD sustains itself after grant funding ends. Four revenue streams. Path from grant-dependent (Year 1) to self-sustaining (Year 3). Contingency plan if revenue grows slower.

Team and credibility (2 documents):

  • grant/team.md — Tom Cranstoun (52 years, BBC, Adobe, Fortune 500). Scott McGregor (co-founder, MX Printworks pilot). Advisory board. Planned hires.
  • grant/fund-criteria-matrix.md — Six grants scored across 13 criteria (innovation, job creation, R&D risk, net zero, export potential, etc.). Shows which grants to apply for first and how to frame each application.

Pitch and communication (2 documents, Rankinized):

  • grant/README.md — The critical document. An investor pitch for grant committees, restructured using Andy Raskin's 5-element sales narrative framework:

    1. The Change — Four AI commerce launches in one week, January 2026
    2. Winners and Losers — The cruise that cost 100x too much vs the retailer that adapted
    3. Promised Land — Scotland in 2028, operating the global verification registry
    4. Magic Gifts — Three obstacles, three gifts (Registry, Signing Engine, MX Specifications)
    5. Evidence — Working technology, two pilots, published track record, the team

  • grant/public-explainer.md — Plain language, zero jargon. Opens with "Something big just happened" (the Change), moves to "The AI that ruined a holiday" (Winners/Losers), paints a picture of a world where AI never guesses (Promised Land), explains REGINALD as "a library for AI" (Magic Gift), and closes with the team (Evidence).

Demo website:

  • An MX-compliant HTML page at mx-outputs/datalake/clients/reginald/reginald-website/index.html. Run with npm run demo, select "reginald". Features:
    • Interactive REGINALD registry lookup (type "Claude Code", "npm", or "git submodule")
    • Before/after comparison (AI guessing vs AI looking it up)
    • Winners and losers cards
    • Environmental impact statistics
    • Schema.org JSON-LD metadata (viewable in-page — this page practices what it preaches)
    • MX compliance badge

The meta observation

This session is itself a demonstration of the problem REGINALD solves.

Maxine (Claude Opus 4.6) built 17 documents and a demo website in one session because she had access to structured, well-organised source material. The technical proposal had YAML frontmatter. The VISION-BRIEF had clear structure. The company information was in known locations with consistent formatting.

When AI has access to structured, findable, trustworthy documentation, it produces correct, useful output at remarkable speed. When it doesn't, it guesses.

The entire REGINALD grant application package was built because the source documentation was MX-compliant. The grant application argues for funding a company that makes documentation MX-compliant for other AI systems.

MX in practice, building the case for MX. Recursive. Self-referential. And it works.
