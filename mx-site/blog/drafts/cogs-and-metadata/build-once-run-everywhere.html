Build Once, Run Everywhere

13 June 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 3 min read

A new client signs. Before anything starts, someone has to learn their CMS, their terminology, their internal sign-off chain, and their idea of what a finished page looks like. That takes weeks. On the second engagement, you start again.

The engagement itself might take a day. The context around it takes a month. There's no shortcut to that, and no tool that removes it. You're not slow because your process is bad. Every engagement is, at its core, a new relationship with a new world.

The Part That Doesn't Get Cheaper

A lot of things in agency work got faster in 2024 and 2025. First drafts, image sourcing, research summaries - these compress. The hours on a statement of work come down.

What doesn't compress is the onboarding. The kickoff meeting where three people have three different answers to "what are we trying to do here". The round of feedback that arrives on a Friday. The one stakeholder who wasn't there when the brief was agreed - that gap isn't a function of how fast you can write.

This is the part services businesses have been trying to solve for thirty years. Adding people makes it worse before it makes it better. Adding tools makes it faster without making it simpler. The only thing that actually helps is when the client can do more of it themselves.

What That Points To

If the constraint is always the client's context - their stack, their vocabulary, their sign-off chain - then the answer isn't to learn it faster. The answer is to build something they can pick up and run on their own.

That's a tools business, not a services one. And those are structurally different.

A services business earns by spending time inside client problems. A tools business earns by removing the need for that time. The two models pull in opposite directions: the more embedded a services firm becomes, the harder it is to extract; the more portable a tools firm's output, the more clients it can reach.

What CogNovaMX Actually Builds

MX is a metadata standard. REGINALD is the infrastructure that makes that metadata machine-trustworthy. The Gathering is the community that governs the standard openly.

None of those are things we do for you. They're things you run.

When an agency deploys MX across a client's content estate, we're not there. The audit pipeline runs on their infrastructure. The REGINALD records come from their own data. The COGs travel with their files, not with us.

The audit is the clearest example. We built it once. Every agency that certifies with us runs it against their own clients. The template adapts; the infrastructure doesn't change. The work happens at their end, with our standard underneath it.

That's what "build once, run everywhere" means in practice.

Why This Matters If You Run an Agency

We don't compete with you. We don't do the work your team does. We build the layer that makes that work auditable and explainable to the machines and regulators reading everything your clients publish.

You bring the relationship and the expertise. We build the rails.

Tools. Training. Documentation. Not a team of people in your Slack doing it for you.
