Self-Interested Platforms Leave the Hard Half Unsolved

27 June 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 8 min read

A post crossed my feed claiming Google's VP of Search Ads is telling CMOs to stop doing the things Google's own algorithm quietly rewards. The claim is partly right. Google does rank links, brand mentions, and click behaviour while telling you to just write good content. That is a real gap between public guidance and internal truth. And it is also the wrong problem to solve.

The actual problem is older and deeper. No platform, no matter how honest, will ever solve the question that matters most once machines start reading your content: where did this come from, and can I verify it has not been altered? That problem is structurally unsolvable by Google because solving it means validating content independent of Google's index. That would be against Google's own business.

That is not cynicism. That is how incentives work.

The Reddit post is right about the gap

Let me start here because the observation is sound. Google does process NavBoost click data while telling you clicks don't matter. Google does run PageRank variants on every document while saying build links at quality. Google does use Chrome interaction data and siteAuthority brand signals while omitting them from public guidance. The 2024 API leak and DOJ testimony confirm this. The gap is real.

A VP of Ads has revenue incentives misaligned with organic visibility. If organic becomes harder, advertisers spend more. That is not a conspiracy. It is how P&Ls work. The guidance gets sanitised not because Google is lying but because the truth - "do the things that actually signal authority, which look a lot like tactics we penalise at spam scale" - invites the spam Google's guidance exists to prevent. The sanitisation is structural. It is also incomplete.

That diagnosis is where the Reddit post stops. And it is where the actual work begins.

But the gap between guidance and signals is not the problem you should solve

Here is the critical move: even if Google became 100% transparent about its ranking signals, even if the VP of Ads published a manifesto listing every module, every weight, every heuristic the algorithm uses, it would solve nothing for you.

Because the game you are playing is ending.

Optimising for Google's signals is optimising for visibility inside Google's index. That visibility matters less every quarter. A procurement officer does not find your contract through Google. A regulator does not discover your policy through a search result. An AI agent training on a dataset does not visit your page first. A compliance officer pulling content into an audit report does not check whether it ranks. Your content is being read in systems Google has no index over, by agents Google does not control, in contexts where Google's ranking algorithm is structurally irrelevant.

You can know NavBoost's exact weighting and still be optimising for the wrong thing if the content you are optimising for travels off the web and into systems where NavBoost has no meaning.

That is the floor. The wall above it is trust.

The structural reason self-interested platforms cannot solve this

Google will never tell you how to build content that can be verified independent of Google Search. OpenAI will never tell you how to make content that can be audited outside of ChatGPT. No platform that profits from being the intermediary between the reader and the content will tell you how to make content that speaks for itself.

That is not because they are dishonest. It is because solving that problem would mean creating the conditions for readers to verify content without using their system. A platform solving the verification problem is a platform making itself optional.

Google's web.dev guidance covers the rendered page. The CMA just forced Google to show publishers AI-search metrics. Google published a guide saying GEO is still just SEO. None of this solves provenance because none of it can, within Google's business model. The question - "who published this, when, and how do I know it has not changed?" - is not one Google Search is in the business of answering. That question assumes readers can verify content without relying on Google's index.

Same story for every platform. OpenAI will tell you how to write for LLMs. It will not tell you how to build content that holds you accountable if the LLM hallucinates or cites you wrong. That would be solving a problem OpenAI creates and has no incentive to fix. Anthropic could commit to never hallucinating citations. It will still not tell you how to build content that survives misuse outside their systems, because that is not a problem Anthropic is in the business of solving.

The incentive gap is not a bug in any individual platform. It is the nature of platforms. A platform's business is being the arbiter, the gatekeeper, the reader. Solving the "content that verifies itself" problem means content no longer needs a gatekeeper. Platforms will not solve that on their own because they cannot profit from it.

The problem that actually changes things

The CMA just changed this. On 3 June 2026 it ordered Google to show publishers disaggregated AI-search metrics, attributed and auditable. It did not order Google to make content verifiable. It ordered Google to report what it did with content in machine-readable form. That is a different problem, and it is the one regulators are now solving for platforms.

The EU AI Act, the European Accessibility Act, and the emerging digital-records regimes expect organisations to produce documentation that is structured, logged, and verifiable on request. They are not asking platforms nicely. They are legislating. The compliance burden is landing on organisations, not platforms. That shifts the work.

Organisations that want to survive the next regulatory wave cannot rely on platforms to tell them what to do. They cannot wait for Google's next guidance update or OpenAI's next best-practice post. They have to build content that carries its own credentials, its own proof of origin, its own audit trail. Not because it makes them visible to Google. Because it makes them defendable to regulators.

That is where MX stops being optional.

What you actually need to do

Write good content. Build authority. Cite real sources. Keep the structure clean. Google's guidance on all of that is sound. Do not rely on Google to tell you how to optimize for the next platform.

Then do the half Google cannot and will not cover. Build content that carries:

  • Provenance. Who published this, when, under what version.
  • Authentication. A way to verify the publisher's identity and that the content has not been altered.
  • Rights and licensing. What machines may do with this content, declared in machine-readable form.
  • Lifecycle. When this document supersedes another, when it is retracted, when it is deprecated.
  • Audit trail. A record of every change, every decision, every AI system that touched it.

None of that helps you rank in Google. All of it helps you survive the next regulatory moment, the next platform shift, the next auditor question.

That is the half self-interested platforms will never cover. That is also the half that actually matters.

Why GEO is a treadmill

GEO is not a strategy. It is a symptom. You are optimising for the visible signal because the platform controls what signals are visible and you have no choice. Every quarter the signal changes because it is linked to one product team's release schedule. You have no leverage over that. You have no way to verify that your optimisation is working beyond what the platform tells you. You have no way to defend your decision if the platform changes the rules next week.

That is treadmill work. You can run faster and still move nowhere.

MX is not a treadmill because it is not dependent on what any single platform decides to reward. Content that carries its own provenance, its own rights declaration, its own audit trail is defensible to any future platform, any regulator, any context. A platform might ignore it. But it cannot revoke it. It cannot rewrite it. It cannot bury it in an algorithm update.

The difference is agency. GEO gives you none. MX gives you the only kind that matters - the kind that does not depend on a platform to prove.

The straightforward argument

If your job is to rank in Google this quarter, optimise for Google's signals. That is a reasonable short-term goal. Google's guidance on page quality, structure, and crawlability is sound. Use it.

If your job is to ensure that the content you publish can survive the next platform shift, can be audited by a regulator, can be verified by any agent that reads it without Google's mediation, then you need a layer no self-interested platform will ever build.

That layer is MX. The verifiable infrastructure is REGINALD. The governance is The Gathering, a community-owned standards body on the W3C model, independent of any vendor.

You need both. The page done right, for the platform. The file done right, for everything that comes after the platform.

The platform will tell you how to do the first. It will never tell you how to do the second because the second is the one that makes the platform optional.

So do not wait for that guidance. Build it yourself. The organisations that do will be the ones that survive the next wave.
