Getting cited by AI is not one goal. It is five.

13 June 2026 · Tom Cranstoun · 5 min read

I track how often AI engines cite sources in my own category, the AI-search and answer-engine tooling space. For a long time I looked at the numbers pooled, one total across every engine. Two weeks ago I stopped, and broke the same dataset down by engine instead. About 19,000 citations, fourteen days, five engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Claude), the same 22 buyer prompts run against each one.

The thing I was not ready for: the engines barely cite the same sources.

The numbers that stopped me

Wikipedia is the second most-cited domain in the whole dataset, somewhere around 950 citations. It is cited by exactly one of the five engines. ChatGPT on its own drags it to second place. The other four basically never pull it. TechRadar at number three has the same shape, ChatGPT only again.

Reddit is the most-cited domain overall, around 1,600, and even it is missing from two of the five. Perplexity and Claude do not pull Reddit in my set at all. YouTube ranks high too, and ChatGPT ignores it.

The only domains all five engines agree on are the vendor and tool sites in the category, the SEMrushes and HubSpots and a couple of the answer-engine tools. I think that is the tell. The cross-engine overlap is mostly brand-name lookups, where every engine fetches the same homepage. For the actual discovery prompts, "best tool for X", "how do I do Y", each engine is off in its own world of sources.

Why this is structural, not a quirk of my category

It would be easy to write this off as one weird vertical. I do not think it is, because the mechanism is general. The five engines sit on different retrieval backends. ChatGPT search leans on one index, Perplexity runs its own index and ranker, Google AI Overviews and Gemini draw on Google's index, Claude's web search reaches through another provider again. Different index plus different ranker plus different, unpublished system prompt equals a different source diet, before anyone touches a single content tactic.

Once you see it that way, the two findings I would actually stake something on stop looking like noise. ChatGPT effectively owning Wikipedia, and Perplexity skipping Reddit, are both the kind of thing different retrieval plumbing predicts. The specific domains in my chart are flavored by my niche. The shape of the disagreement is not.

The agreement being concentrated on brand lookups is the same story from the other side. A navigational query collapses to one canonical answer every backend returns. A discovery query is where the rankers fan out. So the place the engines agree is the place you least need them to, and the place you most need to win, the discovery prompt, is where they agree least.

What it changed for me

"Get cited by AI" stopped being one goal. It is five weakly-overlapping ones.

A page that earns you ChatGPT citations, encyclopedic, editorial, Wikipedia-shaped, can do nothing for Perplexity, which is reaching for a different set entirely. If you are optimizing against a single blended AI-visibility score, you are averaging across five systems that do not agree, and the average ends up describing none of them. You can lift the blended number while losing ground on the one engine your buyers actually use, and the dashboard will not tell you, because the dashboard pooled the thing that mattered.

The honest unit of measurement here is per-engine. Pooling hides exactly the disagreement you need to see.

The honest caveats

This is one category, and a strange one. AI-search tooling is incestuous; the tools all cite each other, which is part of why the vendor homepages are the only cross-engine agreement. Claude also had a smaller response count in this window, so some of its misses are partly just volume rather than a real snub, and I have tried not to over-read those. The two I would defend are the structural ones: ChatGPT and Wikipedia, Perplexity and Reddit.

Where this lands for me, beyond the chart

I build and write about Machine Experience, so I will say where I think this leaves the optimization conversation, and you can take it or leave it.

If your visibility depends on the ranking patterns of five engines that draw from near-disjoint sources, and any one of them can rewrite its system prompt overnight, you are renting visibility from five landlords who do not talk to each other. Chasing a blended score is renting from all five at once and pretending it is one tenancy.

The thing I would rather own is the document. Make it readable by any machine on any backend, carry its own provenance so an engine can trust it without guessing, and give it a canonical home so it can be found and verified wherever it travels. That does not promise you a citation in any specific engine this quarter. It does mean the work is not undone the next time one of the five changes how it retrieves. The per-engine citation chart is a genuinely useful observation of the discovery layer. It is not a strategy, because there is no single thing it measures.

A question for anyone else tracking this

So the real question. Has anyone split their citation data by engine instead of pooling it? Does the near-zero overlap on the top sources hold in your category too, or is mine an artifact of a niche vertical? I am genuinely curious whether per-engine source diets are a general thing or just my data being strange. If you have run the same prompts across engines and kept the cited domains, I would like to compare shapes.
