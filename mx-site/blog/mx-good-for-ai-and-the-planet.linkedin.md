# MX is good for AI, and for the planet

Here is something we watched happen in our own work. A developer asked an AI coding assistant about a command. The command was real and fully documented. The assistant told him it didn't exist. What followed was a troubleshooting cascade across two AI systems that burned around 40,000 tokens to get back to an answer that needed about 200. A 200-to-1 waste ratio, on a single question.

Those wrong answers cost real money to produce. Most people count only the first loss, the bad answer itself, and miss the second, the compute burned getting to it.

I contributed to a recent VC Magazine feature on AI digital identity and trust, and they quoted me in the piece. That line is the part worth pulling out. When a machine can't read your content cleanly, it doesn't stop to ask. It guesses. And every guess costs compute, burns the energy behind it, and risks being confidently wrong.

That's the case for Machine Experience, or MX. MX is metadata that travels with a file and tells a machine what it is, where it came from, and how it's meant to be used. When agents read attested facts instead of inferring them, they hallucinate less, because they have something real to cite. They also make fewer, cheaper model calls, because the structure is explicit and doesn't have to be worked out from scratch on every read.

For a company that shows up two ways. Where you pay by the token, fewer wasted inferences means a lighter bill. I'll be straight: if you're billed by the request instead, the saving is real, but it won't reach your invoice. Underneath the billing, every inference you avoid is compute you didn't run, which is energy you didn't burn.

There's a third reason this matters now, and it's the law.

> **Note:** This page describes regulatory frameworks in general terms only. Nothing here is legal advice. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, organisation type, and use case. Consult qualified legal specialists for guidance specific to your situation.

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026: providers of AI systems that generate text, image, audio, or video have to mark those outputs in a machine-readable format. The European Accessibility Act has been in force since June 2025. The direction is the same in every jurisdiction I watch: regulators want records a machine can read and verify. MX produces that kind of record as a matter of course.

Let me be clear about the limits. MX doesn't grant you compliance; that stays your legal duty. What it gives you is the structured, verifiable evidence those regimes expect you to produce on request.

The part I like most is simple. One of MX's founding principles puts it plainly: "Choose what is better for the planet. Reduce compute, reduce inference, reduce energy." A machine reading a clean, structured file instead of guessing at messy prose pays a smaller energy bill, and that cost, multiplied across every agent that touches your content, adds up. Lower spend, fewer wrong answers, and a lighter load on the grid.
