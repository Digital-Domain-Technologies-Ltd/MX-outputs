Why use MX

Machines now read your content before people do. An AI agent answers a customer's question about you, a search system summarises your page, and a screen reader describes your document. They work from what they can parse and verify. When they can do neither, they guess, and your name is on the answer.

MX is metadata that travels with the file. It records where the file came from, what it is for, and how it may be used, in a form a machine reads directly instead of inferring. The same record works in a web page, a PDF, an image, or a data file, so your content keeps its meaning after it leaves the site that published it.

Agents then quote what you declared, not what they assumed. You also hold a dated record of what you published, which an auditor or a partner can check for themselves. What a machine needs is what assistive technology needs too, and MX builds on the web standards you already use rather than replacing them.

This PDF is a worked example. Inspect it to see the record for yourself.

What inspect does

Inspect opens the MX Inspector and fetches the PDF into your browser, where it is read locally and never uploaded. You see the MX metadata embedded in the file and whether the PDF is tagged for assistive technology. You also see the fingerprint of the source it was built from and the provenance records that travel beside it, which you can follow as an evidence chain.

Making the web, and everything you publish beyond it, work for everyone and everything that uses it.
