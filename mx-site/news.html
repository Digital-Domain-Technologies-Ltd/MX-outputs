Recent Developments

Torvalds Credits Claude Code in a Kernel Patch Series, and Says Who Measured What

12 October 2026 | Ecosystem

Linus Torvalds published an experimental eight-patch series, on a branch of his own Linux tree, that speeds up small reads from the page cache. The series credits Claude Code, working under his direction, with the code, the test programs and the first draft of the cover letter, and states that he ran the measurements himself on his own machine. The disclosure separates what the assistant produced from what the person checked, so a reviewer knows which claims rest on which source. MX records the same split in the file.

Machine-Written Vulnerability Reports Outrun Human Review

8 October 2026 | Ecosystem

Google paused new product reports to its open-source bug bounty on 1 October, saying most automated submissions were not valid. A week later Anthropic launched OSS Scanner, a free scanning service for open-source maintainers, after finding over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities and being able to triage only about 6,000. Its scanner reports are sent without human review, and they say so. A report that states how its finding is known can be routed; one that does not has to be read in full or refused.

Two Justice Department Lawyers Referred for Bar Discipline Over AI Use

17 November 2026 | Governance

The US Justice Department's Office of Professional Responsibility found that two of its lawyers broke their professional conduct rules through AI use and referred both towards possible state bar discipline. One filed an AI-generated brief with fabricated quotations and then gave the court a false account of it; the other uploaded documents to a public AI platform in breach of a protective order. In both cases the missing piece was a record: what produced the content, who checked it, and what the document was allowed to be used for. MX keeps those facts in the file.

AI Translation Reaches a Trillion Words a Month, and the Human Check Becomes Optional

13 October 2026 | Ecosystem

BBC News reports that AI now dominates translation: Google says people translate around a trillion words a month across its products. The Open Knowledge Association, the largest contributor of translated articles to Wikipedia, says skipping the human reviewer has become a serious option. Its own study rated about 6% of errors as critical, and Wikipedia's editors still found mistakes in work it had checked. Machine output now reads fluently, so a reader cannot tell a checked translation from an unchecked one. MX records the source, the translator, and the human-check step in the file, where the reader can see them.

YouTube Music Labels AI-Generated Songs

14 October 2026 | Platforms

YouTube Music now shows a label on the player page for tracks where AI played a significant role, and the labels are coming to the main YouTube app. Labels and distributors declare one of three DDEX-defined values when they deliver music: Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, or No Gen AI. Where a supplier declares nothing, YouTube's own detection decides. Apple Music, Spotify, TIDAL, and Deezer have reached the same small vocabulary, which maps onto the MX provenanceOrigin values; the gap is that YouTube's declaration stays in the delivery feed instead of travelling with the track.

Anthropic Launches Fable 5 and Mythos 5, Then Suspends Foreign Access Within 72 Hours

12 June 2026 | Governance

Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 on 9 June 2026, its most capable models, available on the Claude API, AWS Bedrock, Vertex AI, and Microsoft Foundry. Fable 5 includes safety classifiers that can decline requests and return a refusal response rather than an error. Three days later, a US government export control directive required Anthropic to suspend all access by foreign nationals to both models, citing a discovered jailbreak method for cybersecurity tasks. Anthropic complied while publicly disputing the basis, arguing the vulnerability exists across other deployed models. The event is the first documented case of a government directive disabling a globally deployed AI model overnight.

Visa Plugs Its Payment Network Into ChatGPT

10 June 2026 | Commerce

At its Payments Forum in San Francisco, Visa announced it has embedded its payment network inside ChatGPT. Agents can complete purchases at potentially any merchant that accepts the card: OpenAI supplies the agent, Visa the approval and fraud monitoring. The move follows the retirement of OpenAI's opt-in Instant Checkout and inverts the model, reaching merchants who never integrated anything.

Microsoft Launches MAI Models and Frontier Tuning

2 June 2026 | Platforms

Microsoft AI shipped a family of seven in-house MAI models trained from scratch, alongside Frontier Tuning, which adapts a model inside reinforcement-learning environments wired to a company's real workflows. The trace of real work becomes the training asset; a frontier health model co-built with Mayo Clinic runs first inside the hospital's own environment.
