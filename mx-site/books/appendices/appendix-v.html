Appendix V: Reginald v-next Records Reference

The content cog is the first record type the Reginald registry handles. It is not the only one. Two more record types share the same schema rules, the same canonicalisation, the same signature primitives, and the same registry behaviour — decision records, which describe AI decisions in the form regulators and auditors need to consume, and policy records, which carry regulatory codes as addressable, versioned, signed artefacts rather than moving PDFs. A fourth record type wraps any of these — attestation records, the signed trust layer that lives outside the data record it covers.

This appendix is a field-level reference for the three additional record types. Each section gives the required fields, one worked sample, and a short reasoning paragraph that ties the record back to the architectural rule it depends on: the cog is data; the attestation is the trust wrapper; the registry produces records and serves them; verification is a client-side operation against existing standards.

The deep specification, including optional fields, error responses, query semantics, completeness mechanisms, and the build-sequence dependencies, lives in the v-next REGINALD PRD in canon. This appendix is the book's reference shape: enough to recognise each record, enough to verify a worked example, enough to reason about how the five record types fit together.

1. The decision record

A decision record is what an operator publishes for each AI decision it makes. The record is the event; the input that led to the decision stays with the operator. A regulator opening an inquiry walks from the decision record to the input via the operator, not via the registry.

1.1 Required fields

Field Semantics
contentType Fixed string: decision
cogVersion Semver of the decision-cog schema this record conforms to
decisionId Time-sortable unique identifier (ULID recommended)
operator DID or resolvable identifier of the operator
issued RFC 3339 timestamp of the decision
policyRef Reference to the policy cog in force: registry URI, cog ID, version, optional clause fragment
model Model identifier, version, and reference to the model card
inputHash SHA-256 of the canonicalised input (the input itself stays with the operator)
inputClass Operator-defined category of input
outputSummary Short factual description of the output
humanInLoop Whether human review was required, whether it fired, opaque reviewer reference

Field names follow MX naming binding NDR-02 (camelCase). Signatures over the record live in an accompanying attestation record (§3 of this appendix), never in the decision record itself.

1.2 Worked sample

---
contentType: decision
cogVersion: 1.0
decisionId: 01JC8X7K2N4P5Q6R7S8T9V0W1Y
operator: did:web:example-insurance.com
issued: 2026-05-14T10:32:17Z
policyRef:
  registry: https://reginald.tg.community
  cogId: eu-ai-act-art-12
  version: 1.0.0
  clause: para-1
model:
  id: example-insurance/triage-classifier
  version: 2.3.1
  cardRef: cog:web:example-insurance.com:models-triage-v2-card
inputHash: sha256:8f3a...
inputClass: insurance-claim-routine
outputSummary: Routed to standard review queue
humanInLoop:
  required: false
  fired: false
---

1.3 Reasoning

Every field shown is required; nothing optional appears in the sample, so what the reader sees is the minimum a valid decision record must carry. The operator field resolves to a DID document the publisher controls — the registry does not own the operator identity, only points at it. The policyRef block locates exactly which clause of which version of which policy was in force at decision time, by naming the registry that holds the policy cog. The inputHash is the only thing about the input that ever reaches the registry; the raw input stays with the operator and is recoverable only on the operator's own systems, on demand. The outputSummary is the human-readable verdict, deliberately short so the body of the record can be reviewed in seconds. The record carries no signature: trust apparatus is a separate record (§3 of this appendix). The decision record and its attestation are bound only by the record's ID and content digest, never by an embedded signature field.

2. The policy record

A policy record is a regulatory code published as a cog: stable identity, semver versioning, addressable clauses, currency tracking through the same mechanism content cogs use. Decision records reference policy records by policyRef; that reference resolves to a fixed referent whatever happens to the regulator's website.

2.1 Required fields

Field Semantics
contentType Fixed string: policy
cogVersion Semver of the policy-cog schema this record conforms to
policyId Stable identifier for the policy
policyVersion Semver of this policy version
issuingBody DID or organisation reference of the regulator
jurisdiction ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, or EU, or international
effectiveFrom RFC 3339 date the policy version takes effect
clauses Addressable fragments with fragment ID, title, and body anchor

2.2 Worked sample

---
contentType: policy
cogVersion: 1.0
policyId: eu-ai-act-art-12
policyVersion: 1.0.0
issuingBody: did:web:digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu
jurisdiction: EU
effectiveFrom: 2026-08-02T00:00:00Z
clauses:
  - id: para-1
    title: Record-keeping requirement
    bodyAnchor: record-keeping-requirement
  - id: para-2
    title: Automatic event recording
    bodyAnchor: automatic-event-recording
---

2.3 Reasoning

The policy record is the durable referent that every decision record's policyRef resolves against. policyId plus policyVersion form an identity that does not move when the URL of the regulator's publication changes. effectiveFrom lets a verifier reason about which policy version was in force at a given decision time, which matters whenever a decision is reviewed long after it was made. The clauses array makes individual fragments addressable so a decision record can reference "paragraph 1" by clause: para-1 without ambiguity, and a regulator inquiry can ask "which decisions cited this specific clause" without parsing prose. issuingBody names the regulator's DID; whether that DID has actually signed the policy record, or whether a registry has third-party-published it on the regulator's behalf, is recorded in the accompanying attestation record (§3 of this appendix), not in the policy record itself. The policy record is data; the trust wrapper is separate.

3. The attestation record

An attestation record is produced by the trust layer — the registry — and wraps a cog with cryptographic apparatus. The cog itself is unchanged; the attestation references the cog by ID and digest. A cog can be unattested, attested by one registry, or attested by several different registries, without any change to the cog's schema or bytes.

The attestation record is itself a cog: the first field declares contentType: attestation. The record format is recursive, which is the rule of separation made structural — trust wrappers are also data files conforming to a cog schema.

3.1 Required fields

Field Semantics
contentType Fixed string: attestation
cogVersion Semver of the attestation-cog schema
attests Reference to the cog being attested: cog ID and SHA-256 digest of the JCS-canonicalised bytes
issuedBy DID of the trust-layer operator (the registry instance)
issuedAt RFC 3339 timestamp of attestation
mechanism One of individual or merkleBatch
signature For individual: signature over the digest, algorithm identifier, key reference
batch For merkleBatch: root hash, inclusion proof, batch-root attestation reference

3.2 Worked sample — individual attestation

{
  "contentType": "attestation",
  "cogVersion": "1.0",
  "cogId": "cog:web:reginald.tg.community:atst-01JD3K5N7P9Q1R3S5T7V9W2Y4Z",
  "attests": {
    "cogId": "cog:web:example-insurance.com:01JC8X7K2N4P5Q6R7S8T9V0W1Y",
    "digest": "sha256:7e4d8a3c91b25f6e0a4d2b8c7f3e1a9d6b4c2e8a0f9d7c3b1a5e8d4f2c6b9a3e"
  },
  "issuedBy": "did:web:reginald.tg.community",
  "issuedAt": "2026-05-15T11:00:43Z",
  "mechanism": "individual",
  "signature": {
    "algorithm": "ed25519",
    "keyId": "did:web:reginald.tg.community#sign-2026",
    "value": "z3MvkP9hQ7tR2nL5xK8wB4cF6jH1aD0eY3uT9sN2vM..."
  }
}

3.3 Reasoning — individual

The record's own cogId lives in the registry's namespace because the registry produced it. The attests block is what binds the wrapper to its target: the target's cogId so the verifier can fetch the target, and the SHA-256 digest of the target's JCS-canonicalised bytes so the verifier can confirm the target has not changed since signing. issuedBy is the registry's DID; signature.keyId is the specific verification method inside that DID document holding the public key. To verify, the client resolves the DID, retrieves the key by keyId, and validates the Ed25519 signature over the digest using a standard library. The registry produced this record; the registry does not verify it. Verification is entirely client-side, against existing standards.

3.4 Worked sample — merkle-batch attestation

The per-cog record carries an inclusion proof against a shared batch root:

{
  "contentType": "attestation",
  "cogVersion": "1.0",
  "cogId": "cog:web:reginald.tg.community:atst-01JD3K5N7P9Q1R3S5T7V9W2Y4Z",
  "attests": {
    "cogId": "cog:web:example-insurance.com:01JC8X7K2N4P5Q6R7S8T9V0W1Y",
    "digest": "sha256:7e4d8a3c91b25f6e0a4d2b8c7f3e1a9d6b4c2e8a0f9d7c3b1a5e8d4f2c6b9a3e"
  },
  "issuedBy": "did:web:reginald.tg.community",
  "issuedAt": "2026-05-15T11:00:43Z",
  "mechanism": "merkleBatch",
  "batch": {
    "rootAttestation": "cog:web:reginald.tg.community:batch-01JD3K5N6P8Q0R2S4T6V8W1Y3Z",
    "leafIndex": 4271,
    "proof": [
      "sha256:a1b2c3d4...",
      "sha256:e5f6a7b8...",
      "sha256:c9d0e1f2..."
    ]
  }
}

The corresponding batch-root attestation, signed once, covers the entire batch:

{
  "contentType": "attestation",
  "cogVersion": "1.0",
  "cogId": "cog:web:reginald.tg.community:batch-01JD3K5N6P8Q0R2S4T6V8W1Y3Z",
  "attests": {
    "merkleRoot": "sha256:9f8e7d6c5b4a3210fedcba9876543210abcdef0123456789fedcba0987654321",
    "leafCount": 8431,
    "hashFunction": "sha256"
  },
  "issuedBy": "did:web:reginald.tg.community",
  "issuedAt": "2026-05-15T11:00:43Z",
  "mechanism": "merkleBatch",
  "signature": {
    "algorithm": "ed25519",
    "keyId": "did:web:reginald.tg.community#sign-2026",
    "value": "z8RxKj3mQ1tP9wL4nB7cF2dH5jK6aE0bY3uT8sN1vM..."
  }
}

3.5 Reasoning — batch

The per-cog record carries no signature of its own. Its batch.rootAttestation field names a sibling record — the batch root — that carries the single signature covering the whole batch. The proof array is the sequence of sibling hashes a verifier needs to walk from this leaf up to the root. The batch-root record is what the registry actually signed: one Ed25519 operation across thousands of records rather than one per record. The economics matter at scale; the security does not change, because changing any leaf changes its hash, propagates up, and breaks the root signature.

A verifier validating an individual decision cog fetches three records — the decision cog itself, the per-cog attestation, and the batch-root attestation — and performs three checks: the cog's JCS digest matches attests.digest, the inclusion proof reconstructs the root in batch.rootAttestation, and the Ed25519 signature on the root verifies against the registry's public key. All three checks are standard-library operations on existing primitives. The registry produced these records; the registry does not run any of the verification steps.

4. The provenance record

A provenance record is the chain of steps an audit, render, or pipeline went through to produce an artefact. The record is the event log; the artefact carries a pointer to its own provenance record in its metadata, so a reader holding the artefact alone can extract and walk the chain. The provenance record is structurally a sibling of decision, policy, and attestation records and uses the same canonicalisation, naming, and trust-layer separation.

4.1 Required fields

Field Semantics
$schema URL of the published schema this record conforms to
schemaVersion Machine-readable version string of the schema shape
auditId Stable identifier of the audit or pipeline run the chain belongs to
target Identifier of the artefact the chain is evidence for (URI, file path, or content hash)
startedAt RFC 3339 timestamp of the first write to the chain. Never moves after the first write.
lastWriteAt RFC 3339 timestamp of the most recent write, including every step append.
runRevision Monotonic integer incremented on every re-initialisation of the chain against the same auditId. First write is 1. Step appends do not touch this counter.
parties Array of role-attributed identities accountable for the chain. Each entry carries name, email, identifier, organisation, country, and a controlled role value.
frameworks Array of regulatory regimes the chain claims relevance to. Each entry carries id, title, version, jurisdiction, appliesTo, and a reserved x-<framework> extension namespace.
steps Ordered array of step records. Each step carries stepId, timestamp, agent, outcome, and optional inputs, outputs, intent, humanIntervention, frameworks (per-step), and jurisdictionalEvidence (per-step).

Two flat surfaces sit alongside the structured fields so simple greps work: a single-block responsiblePerson at the top of the record mirrors the auditOperator entry from parties[], and a frameworksCited array is a flat list of identifiers drawn from frameworks[].id.

4.2 Worked sample

$schema: https://mx.allabout.network/schemas/provenance-ai/v2
schemaVersion: "2.0"
auditId: dkd.de-de-2026-05-28-01
target: mx-outputs/audit/2026-05-28/dkd.de-de/dkd-de-de-report.pdf
startedAt: 2026-05-28T08:14:02Z
lastWriteAt: 2026-05-28T09:17:42Z
runRevision: 2
parties:
  - role: auditOperator
    name: Tom Cranstoun
    email: tom.cranstoun@gmail.com
    identifier: https://allabout.network
    organisation: Digital Domain Technologies Ltd (trading as CogNovaMX)
    country: GB
frameworks:
  - id: EU-AI-Act
    version: '2024/1689'
    jurisdiction: EU
    appliesTo: all-steps
  - id: EU-EAA-Directive-2019-882
    version: '2019/882'
    jurisdiction: EU
    appliesTo: accessibility-steps
  - id: US-NIST-AI-RMF
    version: '1.0'
    jurisdiction: 'US (voluntary federal framework)'
    appliesTo: all-steps
steps:
  - stepId: no-upstream-provenance
    timestamp: 2026-05-28T08:14:02Z
    agent: mx.pdf.sh
    outcome: skipped
    intent: chain begins at this render; no signed record of the source pre-existed
  - stepId: pdf-render
    timestamp: 2026-05-28T09:17:42Z
    agent: mx.pdf.sh
    outcome: pass
    inputs:
      sourceMd: dkd-de-de-report.md
      sourceSha: a1b2c3d4e5f6...
    jurisdictionalEvidence:
      EU-AI-Act:
        article: '12'
        claim: automated-log
      US-NIST-AI-RMF:
        function: MEASURE
        category: MEASURE 2.1
responsiblePerson:
  name: Tom Cranstoun
  email: tom.cranstoun@gmail.com
  identifier: https://allabout.network
  role: auditOperator
  organisation: Digital Domain Technologies Ltd (trading as CogNovaMX)
  country: GB
frameworksCited:
  - EU-AI-Act
  - EU-EAA-Directive-2019-882
  - US-NIST-AI-RMF

4.3 Reasoning

The chain is honest by construction. The first step in a freshly initialised chain is no-upstream-provenance with outcome skipped, naming the absence rather than inventing prior history. Every subsequent step records what the pipeline actually did. inputs.sourceSha is the git-content hash of the source bytes, so a verifier with access to the source can compute the same hash with git hash-object and confirm the chain points at one byte-exact version forever. The parties[] array names every accountable role the audit names; the frameworks[] array names every regulatory regime the chain claims as relevant evidence; the per-step jurisdictionalEvidence lets a regulator walk straight from clause to step.

The two streams — AI and deterministic — carry the same parties[], frameworks[], runRevision, and lastWriteAt shapes; they differ in which steps land where. The AI stream records non-deterministic events (LLM calls, multi-agent collectors, human-committed actions) for AI-governance regimes; the deterministic stream records rule-driven events (canonicalisation, render, gate verdicts) for accessibility-conformance regimes. A render step lands in the deterministic stream; the AI stream mirrors the event so an inspector reading the artefact sees the same render under both audiences.

The provenance record is the evidence vehicle the canon paper at mx-canon/ssot/papers/provenance-regime-compatibility.md documents. It is not a compliance grant. Compliance with any specific regime remains a legal duty of the organisation; the record provides the queryable, tamper-evident audit trail every modern regulatory regime expects organisations to be able to produce on inspection.

5. How the five record types compose

The five record types compose without any of them needing to know about the others' internals.

A decision record references a policy record by policyRef. The reference is structural — registry URI, cog ID, version, optional clause — and resolves to whatever policy cog the registry holds under that identifier at that version. The decision record does not embed the policy; it points at it.

A policy record is self-contained. It does not reference decision records. It exists to be referenced by them.

A content cog (the format the chapter discusses at length) is structurally a sibling of the decision and policy records. All three use the same canonicalisation rules, the same field naming convention, the same trust-layer separation. The first field of every cog declares its contentType; everything downstream of that field reads the record against the schema for that type.

An attestation record references whichever cog it attests — content, decision, policy, or another attestation. The attestation is opaque to the type of cog underneath; what it carries is a cog ID, a digest, and a signature, and those three are enough to verify the binding regardless of what kind of cog sits on the other end of the reference.

A provenance record is structurally a sibling of content, decision, and policy records. It does not reference policy records by policyRef; the provenance record is the audit trail of how an artefact came to exist, not the decision-event log. A decision record's inputHash and a provenance record's per-step inputs are the two ways an inquiring regulator walks back to source: the decision record names what the model saw at decision time; the provenance record names what the pipeline did at every step. Both shapes are first-class records in the registry; both can carry attestations of their own through §3 of this appendix.

The shape that falls out is a small graph: cogs of four kinds (content, decision, policy, provenance); attestation records wrapping any of them; an inclusion-proof structure linking batched attestations to a single signed root; and a registry whose only job is to serve the records it has been given. Nothing in that graph depends on the registry interpreting any of it. The trust property the system depends on is the standard cryptographic chain — DID resolution, JCS canonicalisation, Ed25519 verification, RFC 6962 inclusion-proof reconstruction — applied by the verifier, not the registry. The registry that produced the records is structurally interchangeable with any other registry that meets the conformance suite.

A machine-to-machine procurement negotiation shows the composition under load. Software agrees a supply contract with other software, no human in the exchange. A human commands the mandate in advance and signs it: a policy record naming the issuer, the agent it commands, the spend ceiling, the terms that may never be conceded, and an expiry. The agent negotiates inside that mandate, and at the moment it agrees it emits a decision record that references the mandate by policyRef and carries the agreed position, the inputs it weighed, and the path to the number. An attestation record signs each. The four questions a finance team or the counterparty's machine would later ask, who approved the terms, who is accountable, whether the deal broke policy, how the position was reached, resolve by reading the decision against the policy it points at: the agreed price sits under the ceiling, no forbidden clause appears, and the author is named and signed. The human did not stand in the loop, because a machine-speed exchange has no point in it to stand at. They stayed in command, and the signed records carry that command forward through every deal struck under it.

6. Where to read further

The deep specification is the v-next REGINALD PRD in canon: identifier semantics, schema versioning, supersession, the submission API, the query model, the security threat model, the governance of the standards body, the bootstrap problem for first policy cogs, the compliance levels mapped onto the new record types. The PRD is normative for the standard; this appendix is the book's reference shape and stays at the level of what each record is and how they fit together.

The chapter that frames the architecture this appendix references is Chapter 20 of MX: The Protocols — Cogs and Reginald — particularly the Beyond the content cog section. The companion MX Attestation Records and Verification draft note in The Gathering's stream is the corresponding open-standard document for the attestation record specifically.
