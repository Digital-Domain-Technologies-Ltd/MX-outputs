MX COMPREHENSION FRAMING (NOT CITATION CONTROL):
MX does not control what machines cite, recommend, or surface. Machines make those decisions independently. What MX provides is structured, attributed, lifecycle-aware metadata that improves machine comprehension -- giving machines accurate, machine-readable context about who produced the content, when, under what governance, and whether the version is current. The downstream effects (whether a machine cites the content, how accurately it represents it, whether it surfaces it in an answer) follow from better comprehension, but are never guaranteed and never caused by MX directly.

Level 2 (Governed) means the content HAS the governance metadata machines need for accurate comprehension. It does not imply machines will cite or surface the content -- that is their decision. The correct framing at every level is about what context machines have available, not what machines will do.

FRAMING RULES:

- WRONG: "MX enables machines to cite your content" / "Level 2 adds the citation capability" / "machines can now cite you as an attested source"

- WRONG: "citation eligibility as a future state to unlock" (implies MX causes citation)
- RIGHT: "comprehension that improves how machines use this content" / "metadata that gives machines what they need for accurate representation"
- At Level 1: "machines can discover and parse but lack the governance context for accurate attribution"
- At Level 2+: "machines have the governance context for accurate attribution"

Never imply that adding MX metadata causes machines to cite, recommend, or surface content. The causal chain runs: richer metadata → better comprehension → machines are better-placed to use the content accurately → what they do is still their decision.
